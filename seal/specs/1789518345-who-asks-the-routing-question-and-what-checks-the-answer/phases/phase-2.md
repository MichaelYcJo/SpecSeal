## What this phase found

**The correction named the wrong source, and the right one is one file over.**
It said `install.sh`'s header calls *this repository's own `CLAUDE.md`* the
block's one source. It does not: `install.sh` lines 43–47 say the block used
to be read out of that file, that #292 measured the two copies and found
`## Git` 95 % identical with one sentence moved in one and not the other, and
that the source is now `templates/claude-md-block.md`. The repository's
`CLAUDE.md` carries a **generated** copy, regenerated by
`.github/scripts/claude_block.py --write` and checked in CI. So the edit
landed in the template and `CLAUDE.md` was regenerated;
`claude_block.py --check` exits 0. Editing `CLAUDE.md` directly would have
been overwritten by the next regeneration and would have shipped the old
paragraph to every installer.

**Three stale copies of `three axes` survived a green suite, and the case that
was supposed to catch them is what let them through.**
`test_the_skill_asks_every_axis_in_the_first_batch` asserted
`"three axes" in skill` — a presence assertion on a count. After the section
was rewritten to two questions, the string was still in the file, in the
orchestrator's own summary line at the top. The suite stayed green while the
document carried two answers. `skills/implement/SKILL.md`'s pointer sentence
and `agents/smith.md` phase 2 held the other two.

That is the class rather than the instance (§12): **a count pinned by presence
passes on the copy that should have moved**, because the stale copy IS the
string the assertion requires. The case is now
`test_the_skill_asks_the_whole_question_in_the_first_batch` and pins the shape
by name plus the ABSENCE of `two axes`, `three axes` and `three checkboxes`.
Two of the three copies are repaired here; `agents/smith.md`'s is phase 4's,
which is why the absence sweep covers `orchestration.md` and `SKILL.md` and
not yet the agent file.

**S6's four phrases are asserted per box, not per file.** A document can carry
all four words in prose two sections down while the cell a person actually
reads at the moment of answering carries none of them. `labelled_table()`
finds each question by its header pair, so neither is found by position.

**The `Automation` row's unchecked cell had to keep the words `may be
asked`.** S6 names that phrase, and the row rename took the frame's value
`somebody may be asked` out of the table. The cell now reads *this run may
stop to ask — somebody may be asked at minute thirty*, which keeps the
scenario verbatim-true and states the misreading the short value invites.
