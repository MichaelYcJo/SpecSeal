## What this phase found

`flat()` is the whole file. *closed on a fix* and *`fixed` verdict* occur 3, 3,
1 and 6 times in the spec, the template, the skill and `chain_check.py` for
reasons unrelated to the count rule, so reverting the rule in any of those
four left the case green. The protocol says the words once, in the rule, and
that is the one carrier the case ever held. The tuple now holds that one, the
comment says why, and `assert COUNT_RULE_CARRIERS` refuses the emptied tuple
that passed vacuously. The other four carriers keep their corrected sentences
and no pin, which is written where a reader meets the case rather than
claimed away.

**Narrowing was the owner's call and it is #161's first rule applied to the
branch that opened #161.** The alternative — eight hand-anchored substrings
and a 700-character window — would have been one more mechanism written by a
fix pass, and the mechanisms fix passes wrote on this branch produced rounds
5, 6 and 9. A pin that works for the other seven copies is a design question
and is left as one.

**The refusal message is the copy that matters most and it was the last one
corrected.** Three rounds corrected the rule three, three and two copies at a
time, each believing its count complete; the message was reached only when a
round asked *what does a person read when this fires*. It named exactly one
way out — make a record's `Needs a fix` say `yes` — which for the sequence
this branch exists for is a false record. It now names both stops and says
*do not rewrite the row to make this pass*. Seen red first: the planted case
fails against the old message on the *none closing on a fix* assertion.

**`Contract changes` is not `none` this time, and the reason is worth a
sentence.** The message is a string inside the error tuple `stopping_floor`

four things the row exists to name — even though no signature, arity or
return shape did. An AST comparison with docstrings stripped confirms it:
`stopping_floor` is the one changed unit, nothing added or removed. The reach
is `main` and the case that runs every real record, as round 8's correction
of round 7's cell already established.

**Eight, not six.** `f187b39` wrote the count rule in the spec twice, the
template, the skill, the protocol, and `chain_check.py` three times. R12
records the eight by coordinate, so the next correction has a list rather
than a count.

**The three records that claimed too much are corrected in place, each
